Things about space that sound made up (but aren't)

I genuinely had to double-check most of these when I was writing them.

☀️

The Sun makes up 99.86% of the solar system's mass

Everything else — all 8 planets, every moon, every asteroid, every comet — accounts for just 0.14% of the total mass. Jupiter makes up most of that 0.14%. Earth is basically a rounding error.

🌑

A day on Venus is longer than a year on Venus

Venus rotates so slowly that it takes 243 Earth days to spin once on its axis, but only 225 Earth days to orbit the Sun. So a Venusian day is longer than a Venusian year. And it spins backwards.

🌊

There's a planet that rains glass sideways

HD 189733b is a deep blue exoplanet where temperatures reach 1,000°C and winds blow at 8,700 km/h. Silicate particles form glass in the atmosphere and are flung around at supersonic speeds. Not somewhere you'd want to visit.

🔇

Space is completely silent

Sound needs a medium to travel through. In the vacuum of space there are virtually no particles, so sound waves can't propagate. Explosions in space would be completely silent. All those movie sound effects are lies.

🫧

Neutron stars are absurdly dense

A neutron star is the collapsed core of a massive star — typically about 20km across but containing more mass than the Sun. A teaspoon of neutron star material would weigh about 10 million tonnes.

🌌

We can't see most of the universe

Normal matter makes up only about 5% of the universe. The other 95% is dark matter and dark energy, which we can't directly observe. We know they exist because of how gravity behaves, but we have no idea what they actually are.

Just how far away is everything?

One of the hardest things to wrap your head around is the actual scale of the solar system. If you shrunk the Sun down to the size of a football (about 22cm), here's where everything else would be:

If the Sun were the size of a football (22cm diameter)

Mercury57.9 million km24 metresA grain of sand
Venus108 million km45 metresA pea
Earth150 million km63 metresA pea
Mars228 million km95 metresA small grape
Jupiter778 million km324 metresA large marble
Saturn1.43 billion km595 metresA marble

Neptune4.5 billion km1.9 km awayA small marble

So if the Sun is a football sitting on the centre spot at Ashton Gate, Neptune would be somewhere near the M32 motorway junction. And the nearest star (Proxima Centauri) would be in New York.
